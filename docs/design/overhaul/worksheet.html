The Umbrella Stand

Every rainy morning, a row of yellow umbrellas waits outside Hsin-Ming Station. Nobody sells them. Nobody guards them. A small sign simply reads, “Take one. Bring it back when the sun comes out.”

The stand was started by Mrs. Lin, a retired teacher who had once been caught in a storm without an umbrella. A stranger shared his with her that day. Not only did he walk her to the station, but he also refused to take anything in return. She never forgot it.我的註記　學生常把 refuse 和 reject 搞混，想多一題辨析。

At first, her neighbors laughed at the idea. “The umbrellas will all be gone in a week,” they warned. In the first month, twelve of the thirty umbrellas did disappear. However, something unexpected followed. Not only did most of the umbrellas return, but new ones also began to appear: blue, green, even one with cartoon cats.我的註記　轉折句。上次這裡停太久。

Today the stand holds more umbrellas than it did at the start. Mrs. Lin says trust is like an umbrella: it is only useful when it is open.
